# int8×int8 below 9 products — existence and minimality of a 6-product construction

Generated by `analysis/loose_weights.py`, `analysis/verify_six.py`, and `analysis/loose_minrank.py`.
All arithmetic is exact (int/Fraction). **The 6-product construction was verified for all 65,025 pairs (A,B)**.

## 1. Correcting the premise — 9 is not a lower bound

Proposition 6 gives a lower bound of 3 on the **number of planes** used to encode A in FP4, not on bilinear rank.
With r products, A ↦ (P_1(A),…,P_r(A)) must be injective, or B=1 gives a contradiction; hence 15^r ≥ 255 → r ≥ 3.
If the planes p_k were linearly independent, λ_k Q_k(B) = c_k B would be forced, and Q_k(B) could not stay in S.
The planes must therefore be linearly dependent: d = dim span{p_k} ≤ r−1. Injectivity acts in d dimensions, so 15^d ≥ 255 → d ≥ 3.

**→ Counting gives the lower bound r ≥ 4. The value 9 is the direct product count, not a lower bound.**

Reconstruction: AB = Σ_i w_i²·P_ii + Σ_{i<j} w_i w_j·(P_ij − P_ii − P_jj)

## 2'. Generalizing planes with (u,v) — coverage increases from 148 to **172**

Planes need not be pair **sums**: with **f_ij = u·a_i + v·a_j**, the identity holds for arbitrary (u,v)
with uv≠0:

```
P_ij = (u a_i + v a_j)(u b_i + v b_j) = u² P_ii + uv (a_i b_j + a_j b_i) + v² P_jj
→ a_i b_j + a_j b_i = (P_ij − u² P_ii − v² P_jj)/(uv)
→ AB = Σ_k λ_k P_k,  λ_ij = w_i w_j/(u v)
                     λ_ii = w_i² − Σ_{j>i} w_i w_j u_ij/v_ij − Σ_{j<i} w_j w_i v_ji/u_ji
```

1/(uv) is a rational epilogue coefficient, so (P1) is preserved. **Clearing denominators** gives
`D·AB = Σ n_k P_k`. The FP32 accumulators contain exact integers, allowing reconstruction in
int32/int64 followed by exact division by D.

Solutions with maximum coverage (**each verified exactly for all 119,025 pairs** by `analysis/verify_uv6.py`):

## 4. Why 6 is minimal (scope of the negative results)

### Relation to evaluation-point methods (interpretation revised 2026-08-18)

With evaluation-point planes α_k=(1,x_k,x_k²,…), the (i,j) entry of Σ_k λ_k α_k β_k^T depends only on
i+j, forming a Hankel matrix. The target w v^T has rank 1. A rank 1 Hankel matrix satisfies
h_{i+j}=w_i v_j, forcing **w and v to be geometric sequences with the same ratio (powers of a radix)**.

**The statement that constructions attaining the lower bound must be interpolation based is already known as Winograd's theorem; it is not a new theorem.** Here it explains the role of free weights:
**FP4 storage constraints prevent the minimum rank from being attained; paying for 1 additional product admits free-weight solutions outside the evaluation-point form.**

Also, **L_A+L_B−1 is not a lower bound in this setting**: the target is a rank 1 matrix decomposition, so
without storage constraints r=1 suffices. The relevant lower bound comes from counting, |S|^{r−1} ≥ 2X+1.
Ratios to “L_A+L_B−1” in figures and tables indicate **potential product reduction**, not a ratio to a lower bound.

The satisfying weights have degenerate arrangements (equal weights, fixed ratios, etc.), and legal tuples fail to cover 127.
**At r=6 these constraints disappear.**

## 6. Excluded mechanisms

| Mechanism | Finding | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Symmetric substitution (x=a+d, y=ad) | **Structurally excluded** | A has shape M×K and B has shape K×N, so A+B cannot be formed. Even inside a limb decomposition, a plane must be a linear combination of only A limbs or only B limbs; this is the bilinear constraint. Only the symmetric product A=B is an exception |
| Per-plane scale s / parity / mod-3 | **No benefit (already included)** | Implemented by extracting a per-plane gcd. All g∈{1,2,3,4,6} were swept; maximum X always occurs at g=1. The half-integer lattice 2^k·S corresponds to integers whose odd part is 1 or 3, another description of the same gcd extraction |
| Toom-3 (5 products) | **Excluded by (O1)** | Maximum X=102, as above. It fails before (O2) matters |
